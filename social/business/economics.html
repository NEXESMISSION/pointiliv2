The platform bill

The one cost that does scale

The counter screen asks a question every 0.9 seconds

The QR screen polls /api/qr/state on a 900 ms interval the whole time it is open, and since the owner's app now opens straight onto that screen, a shop with a tablet on the counter runs it all day. That is by design, and it is what makes a stamp feel instant. It is also the only line in this business that grows with every shop you sign.

3600 s ÷ 0.9 s  =  4,000 calls an hour, per open screen
× 10 hours × 30 days  =  1,200,000 calls per shop per month
at 100 shops  =  120 million function calls a month
Supabase egress ≈0.5 KB each  =  600 MB per shop — the 5 GB free tier is 8 shops
The number to watch

At a hundred shops this is plausibly $80–150 a month in serverless invocations and compute, against $317 of revenue at that size. A third of your gross margin, spent asking a database whether anything happened.

The fix is small, and worth doing before the tenth paying shop rather than the hundredth: poll at 900 ms for twenty seconds after a mint or a stamp, then fall back to four seconds while nothing is happening. Nobody standing at a counter notices, and the bill divides by four. A realtime subscription removes the polling entirely, at the cost of a connection ceiling you would then have to watch instead.

Margin

96% gross, and that is the least interesting number here

You take payment by bank transfer, D17 or cash, so no processor takes a cut — no 3%, no fixed fee per transaction. Sign-in is a phone number and a password stored as an email alias, so no SMS is sent to log anybody in. Both are quietly worth more than they look: a card processor and an SMS gateway would between them take 5–20% of a 120 TND subscription.

The uncomfortable line

On the pessimistic end of every row, year one loses money on every shop you sign. The business is entirely in year two — in the renewal, which costs almost nothing to earn. Everything that matters is therefore churn, not acquisition.

renew 60%  lifetime = 120 ÷ (1 − 0.60) = 300 TND a shop
renew 80%  lifetime = 120 ÷ (1 − 0.80) = 600 TND a shop
cost to win 35–60 TND  →  5× to 17× back on the visit

One thing rescues the cash flow: you are paid a year up front. The 120 TND arrives on the day the rep walks out, and the rep cost 35. That is why annual prepay, not monthly billing, is the right shape for this market — and why the 6-month plan at 80 TND, which is more expensive per month, should stay the awkward option rather than become the default.

What the shapes mean

Alone is you, part-time, four shops a month: it never loses money and it never becomes a company. Full-time is you selling every day. With a rep hires one person in month six. The cumulative line never goes backwards, but the month does: in month six the salary is bigger than the shops it signs, and that is the thinnest month in the table. Drop the renewal dial below 50% and watch which of the three still stands up.

What I would change

Four moves, in the order they pay

  • Charge a 50 TND setup fee. It pays for the visit on the day of the visit, it makes year one profitable on its own, and a one-off fee is far easier for a Tunisian shop owner to accept than a higher subscription. Highest return on this list, and it costs one column in the database.
  • Fix the poll before shop ten. 900 ms while something is happening, four seconds while nothing is. Nobody feels it, and the only cost that scales with your shop count divides by four.
  • Add a tier above, don't raise the floor. Keep 120 TND as the door. Put multi-branch, staff accounts, a data export and customer messaging in a second plan at 300–360 TND. The shops that grow will pay it; the shops that would have refused 200 TND still come in at 120.
  • Make renewal feel automatic. Everything above depends on the renewal rate. A reminder at day 335 carrying the owner's own numbers — «رجعولك 47 حريف هالعام» — and a D17 link is worth more than any feature you could build instead.
What could actually kill it

Five risks, ranked

1 · Churn you cannot see coming

At 120 TND a year you cannot afford to win a shop twice. If renewal lands under 50%, this is a job rather than a business. You will not know the number until October 2027 — unless you start measuring what predicts it now: shops whose stamp count falls month over month.

2 · Password reset does not work in production

The reset flow sends a six-digit code by SMS and no SMS provider is configured, so the code is generated and never delivered. Today you fix it by hand from the admin. At fifty shops that is a phone call a week; at five hundred it is a person. A gateway costs roughly 0.20 TND a message — fine for resets, ruinous if you ever decide to SMS customers.

3 · You are on a Hobby plan

Vercel's Hobby tier is for non-commercial use. The day you take the first dinar, the deployment sits outside the terms it runs under. 63 TND a month fixes it.

4 · Collection is a person, not a system

Bank transfer, D17 and cash cost 0% in fees and about ten minutes each in attention. At a thousand shops on yearly plans that is 83 payments a month to confirm by hand, and every one that slips becomes churn nobody chose.

5 · One person knows everything

One founder holds the code, the database password, the Vercel account and the customer relationships. No amount of margin survives that person being unavailable for a fortnight.
